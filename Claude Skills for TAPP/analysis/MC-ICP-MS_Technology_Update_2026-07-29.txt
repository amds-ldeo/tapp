================================================================================
COLLISION/REACTION CELLS ON MC-ICP-MS — VERIFICATION, DECISION, AND CHANGES MADE
Prepared 2026-07-29
================================================================================

YOUR QUESTION
-------------
Some modern MC-ICP-MS instruments have collision cells (you named Nu Instruments
Sapphire and Thermo Fisher Neoma). Should the collision-cell-related fields in
our TAPPs — currently tagged Q-ICP-MS-only — also apply to MC-ICP-MS? And are
there other similar changes needed given advancement in ICP-MS technology?

SHORT ANSWER
------------
Yes, confirmed and implemented. Six of the seven collision/reaction-cell (CRC)
fields now apply to Q-ICP-MS AND MC-ICP-MS (not SF-ICP-MS). The seventh field,
Signal Collection Mode, correctly stays Q-ICP-MS-only — the reasoning is below,
and it matters, because it's the one place your intuition ("collision cells ->
extend to MC") could have over-extended into something that doesn't actually
transfer. Files changed: Horstwood TAPP (v5->v6), Solution MC-ICP-MS TAPP
(v4->v5), the experiment report (docx), and the development log. LA-Q/SF-ICP-MS
TAPP was NOT changed (explained below).


================================================================================
PART 1 — VERIFICATION (web research performed this session)
================================================================================

1. Nu Instruments Sapphire — CONFIRMED, real and current.
   Dual-path MC-ICP-MS: a conventional high-energy multi-collector path plus a
   separate low-energy path routed through a hexapole collision/reaction cell
   (CRC), operated in modes analogous to a quadrupole ICP-MS/MS (no-gas / KED /
   DRC-style). Nu's own materials describe 170+ units installed worldwide, so
   this is not a lab prototype — it's a mainstream commercial instrument. A
   further variant, Sapphire XD, extends this to a dual-path "MC-ICP-MS/MS."

2. Thermo Fisher Neoma MS/MS — CONFIRMED, real and current.
   Also a hexapole CRC-equipped MC-ICP-MS, with two operating modes (full
   transmission vs. collision/reaction cell mode). It additionally has a
   "pre-cell mass filter" upstream of the cell — but this is architecturally
   NOT a quadrupole. It is a double Wien filter (a crossed electric/magnetic
   field velocity selector), producing a trapezoidal, comparatively
   low-resolution mass "window" that is set as a percentage of the maximum
   magnetic field, rather than scanning/hopping between discrete mass
   positions the way a quadrupole pre-filter does. This distinction is exactly
   why one of the seven fields (Signal Collection Mode) does NOT get extended
   to MC-ICP-MS — see Part 2.

3. Single-collector SF-ICP-MS (Thermo Element XR, Nu AttoM) — CONFIRMED, no CRC
   adoption found.
   No evidence that any current single-collector sector-field instrument has
   added a collision/reaction cell. Sources consistently frame high-resolution
   SF-ICP-MS and collision-cell Q-ICP-MS/MS as two alternative, competing
   approaches to interference removal — a resolving-power strategy vs. a
   chemical/kinetic filtering strategy — not as a spectrum where SF has also
   picked up CRC hardware. This was checked twice (once broadly, once
   specifically targeting Element XR / Nu AttoM) with consistent results. No
   change was made for SF-ICP-MS.


================================================================================
PART 2 — DECISION AND REASONING
================================================================================

The seven CRC-cluster fields (all originally added to LA-Q/SF-ICP-MS TAPP,
then imported into the Horstwood test TAPP tagged "Q-ICP-MS only"):

  1. Collision/Reaction Cell (CRC) Configuration   -> RETAGGED to Q + MC
  2. Collision Gas Type                             -> RETAGGED to Q + MC
  3. Collision Gas Flow Rate                         -> RETAGGED to Q + MC
  4. Cell Exit Discrimination Voltage                -> RETAGGED to Q + MC
  5. Reaction Gas Type                                -> RETAGGED to Q + MC
  6. Reaction Gas Flow Rate                           -> RETAGGED to Q + MC
  7. Signal Collection Mode                           -> LEFT Q-ICP-MS-only

Fields 1-6 describe the cell itself and its gas chemistry (is one installed,
what mode, what gas, what flow rate, what voltage). Nothing about that
description depends on whether the collector array downstream is a single
detector (Q-ICP-MS) or a multi-collector array (MC-ICP-MS) — a hexapole CRC is
a hexapole CRC either way, and Sapphire/Neoma MS/MS both physically have one.
So these six generalize cleanly.

Field 7, Signal Collection Mode, describes something different: HOW the mass
spectrometer collects signal across masses over time — sequentially
("peak hopping," jumping between preset mass positions and dwelling at each
one) or by continuous scanning. This is a concept that only makes sense for an
instrument with ONE detector collecting one mass at a time. MC-ICP-MS's whole
point is a fixed array of Faraday cups (or ion counters) collecting many
masses AT THE SAME TIME — that doesn't change just because a CRC sits upstream
of the collector array. A CRC-equipped MC-ICP-MS still does genuinely
simultaneous multi-collection; it never "peak hops" or "scans." So this field
correctly stays Q-ICP-MS-only, and I added a sentence to its Description
explaining why, rather than silently leaving it unchanged (so a future reader
doesn't wonder whether this was an oversight).

Worth flagging on its own: Neoma's double-Wien-filter pre-cell mass-window
selector is a genuinely new concept with NO corresponding field anywhere in
any TAPP. It isn't "Signal Collection Mode" (it doesn't scan or hop — it
selects a static mass window before the cell) and it isn't the same mechanism
as a Q-ICP-MS/MS's quadrupole pre-filter either. I did not add a field for
this now — see Part 3 for why, and a concrete proposal you can decide on.

PART 4 — OTHER CHANGES DUE TO ICP-MS TECHNOLOGY ADVANCEMENT?
================================================================================

Yes — one finding (ToF) is a near-exact parallel to the collision-cell case
(a technology quietly crossing from one instrument class into another, with
existing fields becoming inapplicable rather than just imprecise); one
(spICP-MS) is a bigger, genuinely-open judgment call; the rest are a shorter
list worth being aware of but not urgent. None of these were implemented —
all are flagged for your decision, same as the Neoma pre-cell-filter finding
above.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 CONCRETE FINDING — ICP-ToF-MS: existing fields are not just imprecise for
    it, they're categorically the wrong concept, and ToF is now spreading
    into MC-ICP-MS territory too.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
As noted above, LA-Q/SF-ICP-MS TAPP already lists "LA-ICP-ToF-MS" and
"Time-of-flight (ICP-ToF-MS)" as allowed values in two controlled-list fields,
without the rest of the schema actually supporting that instrument type.
Confirmed why this matters, via TOFWERK and Nu Instruments specifications and
a peer-reviewed LA-ICP-ToF-MS imaging comparison:

A ToF instrument extracts and records a COMPLETE mass spectrum on every
extraction ("pusher") event — tens of thousands of times per second (e.g.,
83.3 kHz on the TOFWERK icpTOF-S2) — rather than dwelling on one mass at a
time. There is no "dwell time per mass" and no "peak hopping vs. scanning"
choice, because every mass is captured every cycle by construction. The
fields that WOULD actually describe a ToF acquisition, and don't exist in any
TAPP right now:
  - Extraction/pusher frequency (Hz or kHz)
  - Spectral recording/averaging rate (how many raw extractions are summed
    into one output spectrum — replaces "dwell time per mass" as the
    integration-time concept)
  - Mass range covered per pulse (e.g., ~7-275 amu simultaneously)
  - Mass resolving power (typically ~2500 standard; >4000 with collisional
    damping/cooling, itself a duty-cycle tradeoff worth a field)

Notably, this is not confined to LA-ICP-MS: Nu Instruments' Vitesse is a ToF
instrument explicitly marketed for isotope-ratio work — i.e., the same kind
of "one architecture crossing into a different instrument class" pattern as
Sapphire/Neoma's collision cells crossing into MC-ICP-MS, just for a
different underlying technology. At least three vendors now sell ICP-ToF-MS
(TOFWERK icpTOF, Nu Vitesse, GBC ICP-oTOFMS), so this isn't a fringe case.
Recommendation: this is a stronger candidate for near-term action than
anything else in this section, since it's a self-inflicted inconsistency
(inviting a Technique value the rest of the schema can't actually serve)
present in BOTH LA-Q/SF-ICP-MS and, if Vitesse-style use grows, potentially a
future MC-ICP-MS revision too. Suggest treating it as its own follow-up
discussion rather than folding it into this pass.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 A bigger, genuinely open question — spICP-MS / single-cell ICP-MS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is real and actively growing, not a fringe technique: ISO/TS 19590
(spICP-MS nanoparticle characterization) was reissued in August 2024, and
current growth areas include microplastics analysis and single-cell
metallomics/toxicology work (e.g., 2024 studies on metal heterogeneity in
individual cells) alongside the established nanoparticle-characterization use
case.

Its acquisition paradigm is genuinely different from steady-state solution
work: microsecond-to-low-millisecond dwell times (literature converges on
≤100 µs for accurate particle sizing), particle-event detection (background
vs. signal discrimination) in place of concentration averaging, and a
transport-efficiency calibration step (typically the particle-frequency
method against reference nanoparticles, ~45-55% typical) with no equivalent
in solution nebulization work at all.

Open question, not a recommendation: this runs on the SAME physical hardware
as solution ICP-MS (quadrupole, increasingly ToF too) — just in a different
acquisition mode — so it's genuinely arguable either way whether it deserves
its own TAPP (the way this project split MC-ICP-MS from Q/SF, and the way
LA-ICP-MS is split from solution-introduction work) or whether it belongs as
a specialized mode/flag within Solution Q-ICP-MS TAPP. I don't think this is
mine to decide unilaterally — flagging it as a real gap and a real choice for
you.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Shorter list — other 2020s-era hardware changes, lower priority / lower
    verification depth
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Mini/conical torch geometry (e.g., Shimadzu ICPMS-2030/2040/2050
  "Mini-Torch"): a 2-tube conical torch design triples gas velocity, cuts
  argon consumption by ~70%, and allows ~50% less RF power. A genuine
  plasma-source hardware change; current "Torch Type" fields (free text) could
  technically capture this today via "Other: specify," but there's no
  controlled-list awareness of it.
- 10^13 ohm Faraday amplifiers for MC-ICP-MS: originated as custom lab
  modifications around 2011-2014, but is increasingly a standard factory
  option on current instruments (Neoma, Sapphire, Nu Plasma Vulcan) for
  ultra-low-signal isotope ratios, trading off slower amplifier response/decay
  time. Same "niche-to-mainstream spread" pattern as the collision-cell case,
  though the underlying idea predates the 2020s. Already partially covered:
  Solution MC-ICP-MS TAPP's "Faraday Cup Amplifier Resistor Values" field
  already allows recording 10^11/10^12/10^13 ohm per cup — this looks
  adequately captured already, just noting it as context.
- ICP-MS/MS (tandem/triple-quad, e.g., Agilent 8900, Thermo iCAP TQ):
  commercialized mid-2010s but became the de facto high-end quadrupole
  standard through the 2020s. True mass-shift mode needs both a Q1
  (pre-filter) mass setting and a Q2 (analytical) mass setting recorded, plus
  an on-mass-vs.-mass-shift flag per analyte — our current CRC/Reaction Gas
  fields capture what gas is used but not this specific two-quadrupole
  mass-relationship concept. Possible small gap, not verified in depth this
  pass.
- Multi-ion-counting (MIC) detector arrays with RPQ energy filters: this is
  evolutionary (established since 2000s Neptune-era MC-ICP-MS, just with
  more channels on newest instruments), not a new concept — already
  reasonably covered by "Faraday Cup Array Configuration"'s existing mention
  of ion counters. Lowest priority of this list.
- Checked for and found no solid evidence of non-argon plasma sources (e.g.,
  nitrogen plasma) reaching commercial reality — not flagging this as a gap.
